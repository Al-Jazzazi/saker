# Capstone Context: SAKER

## Project Overview
SAKER (Step-to-Assist Knee Exoskeleton Robot) is a rehabilitation capstone focused on helping people with lower-limb paralysis after stroke regain safer and more functional walking ability. The project aims to support gait assistance on the affected side and improve mobility outcomes during recovery.

## Why This Project Matters
- Improve patient independence and quality of life.
- Enable earlier mobilization during rehabilitation.
- Reduce the physical burden of walking during recovery.
- Support better clinical throughput by improving rehab efficiency.

## Problem Context
Many stroke patients with flaccid or weak lower-limb control struggle with safe foot clearance, stable stepping, and repeated gait training. Existing options can be limited by comfort, setup complexity, consistency, or fatigue effects over time. SAKER is intended to provide a practical assistive approach within a clinical rehab workflow.

## Scope of the Capstone
- Develop an assistive lower-limb exoskeleton concept for stroke rehabilitation.
- Integrate sensing and control logic to support step assistance.
- Evaluate usability and potential rehabilitation impact through prototype and trial phases.
- Keep safety, comfort, and clinical practicality as core constraints.

## Expected Impact
- Better assisted gait practice for patients with limited limb control.
- Potential reduction in recovery friction during early rehab.
- A foundation for future clinical trials and translational development.

## Project Path (High Level)
1. Prototype development
2. Internal/investigator testing
3. Iterative revisions
4. Required approvals
5. Patient-facing trials
6. Longer-term IP and productization exploration
